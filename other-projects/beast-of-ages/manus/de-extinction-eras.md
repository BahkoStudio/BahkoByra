That animal is a thylacine, and you are standing here because people are trying to bring it back. A company called Colossal Biosciences, real labs, real money, has put it on a list with the woolly mammoth and the dodo. So we are going to walk backwards, one animal at a time, into the world each of them came from. Four stops. Every stop wants you dead faster than the one before it.

One warning before the first number, and it covers all four. Nobody has run the experiment. Every number in this video is a guess. What the guess rests on you can look up.

This first world takes its time with you. Here is how it starts.

The thylacine moves at dusk and through the night. Which is exactly when you stop working.

Try it tonight. Kill the light in your room and look around. For the first few seconds you see almost nothing. Your eyes need about half an hour in the dark before they are any use to you. Flick the light on once and that clock starts over from zero. At home that is a small annoyance. Here it means the hours when this forest is most awake are hours you have to spend blind.

So how long do you last? Three weeks.

And what finishes you has no teeth in it at all.

It is the water in your clothes. Wet cold pulls heat out of you far faster than dry cold does, which is why people go hypothermic on days nowhere near freezing. You are soaked. Nothing you own is going to dry out here. You also cannot tell which plants around you feed a person and which empty one out, and you get one try at guessing wrong.

Look past the trees and it gets stranger. There are farms on this island, and fences full of sheep. For twenty one years the government here paid people money for every dead thylacine they carried in. More than two thousand of those payments went out. That is a big part of why the coughing behind you is so rare now. The last one anyone will ever record is in a zoo in Hobart tonight, and it has weeks left.

Three weeks is the best score you will get all video. And you got it in a world that still has roads.

So what kills you in a place where nothing is hunting you at all?

The first thing here is the noise. Warm rain hitting a million leaves at once, all around you, a sound with no edges to it. Under that, close, something is breathing beside your knee. Slow. Level. It has not moved.

You look down.

A bird comes up past your waist, close enough to touch. It watches you with the calm of an animal that never learned a reason to run. You are on Mauritius, a green speck of rock in the Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar, and the year is 1662. That bird is a dodo.

Forget the cartoon. That waddling clown was built from sailors' sketches and badly stuffed museum birds. Leon Claessens and Julian Hume measured the real skeletons. They described a bird built for moving through forest, quick on its feet, nothing like the beanbag in your head.

The calm should worry you more than teeth would. Mauritius had no native land mammals except bats, so nothing here ever grew up careful. Two days in, neither are you. You stop looking behind you. That was the habit keeping you alive.

Your body has a harder problem, and it sits just under your ribs. There is water in every direction and you cannot drink a drop of it. Your kidneys can only push salt out up to a certain concentration, and seawater sits above that line. Swallow it and you lose more water than you gain. Sailors died of thirst while floating on an ocean, and your kidneys are the same model theirs were.

So you wait for rain. Rain comes. Cyclones come with it. Then the rats and pigs the ships left behind find your camp, the same way they found dodo nests.

Eleven days.

Now step back much further. What kills you in a place that never has to touch you?

Your fingers stop reporting back first. Then your cheeks go stiff, and your breath freezes on your collar and stays there. Dust gets in your teeth. The wind comes from one direction all day and it does not stop, not once. Grass as hard as wire runs flat away from you to the edge of the world.

Then a shape the size of a truck walks past, unhurried, hair hanging down to its knees.

This is the mammoth steppe, twenty thousand years ago, the largest habitat on Earth at the time, running from Spain across Siberia into Canada. The pyramids are not built for another fifteen thousand years. Nothing on this horizon was made by anyone. That shape is a woolly mammoth.

Everyone pictures walls of blue ice. The ice sheets were real, and they sat far to the north, while most of this world was open pasture.

Look at the mammoth's ears. They are no bigger than your hands. Elephants today carry ears like sails, because getting rid of heat is their whole problem. Every animal here is built the other way round. And you are wearing skin.

Cold air hits your forearms and the skin pulls up into little bumps. Each bump is a tiny muscle standing one hair on end. On a furred animal that lifts the coat and traps a layer of warm air against the body. On you it achieves nothing, because the coat those muscles were built to lift is gone. Your body is still following instructions written for an ancestor who had one.

You want fire, so you look for a tree. There are no trees. People here burned bone and dung instead. At Mezhyrich in Ukraine, people built the walls of their homes out of mammoth bones.

You cannot eat your way out of the cold either. Kill something lean, eat all day, and you still fade, because your body cannot run on protein alone without fat. Everything out here has fat on it. Nothing out here intends to give you any.

Nine hours. The light is already going.

One stop left. So what is faster than the cold, and already knows exactly where you are?

Something smells wrong. Heavy and black, like fresh road tar on a hot day. The grass is dry and yellow and taller than your knees, and every blade of it leans the same way. You take a step and your boot does not come with you.

You pull it free with a sound like a mouth opening.

You are in North America, forty thousand years ago, standing in what will one day be Los Angeles. Count backwards from your mother to her mother, and keep going. You need about fifteen hundred of them, one behind the other, before you reach this grass. That line would leave the room, leave the building, and keep going down the street. Those black patches under your feet are why we know so much about this place.

And for the last hour, something has been walking parallel to you inside the grass, matching your pace. You have not seen it once.

You are picturing a wolf the size of a horse. Wrong. A dire wolf stood about as tall as a big grey wolf, just heavier through the jaw and the shoulders. In 2021 a team led by Angela Perri pulled DNA out of dire wolf fossils and published it in the journal Nature. Their line had split away from grey wolves millions of years earlier than anyone expected, with no sign the two ever interbred. They only looked like family.

They also did not hunt alone. Over the years, more than four thousand dire wolves have been dug out of these pits, more than any other large mammal there. American lions and sabre toothed cats came up out of it too. Every one of them walked in after prey that could not leave. Out here, you are the prey that cannot leave. You have no claws, and nothing on you that bites.

Now check your own eyes. Shine a light at a dog at night and the light comes straight back at you. Yours never do that. Dogs and wolves carry a mirror behind the retina, and humans were never issued one, because our ancestors did their living in daylight. At sunset this ground stops belonging to you.

Then the grass moves, and watch what your body does about it. Before you have decided anything, you go completely still. Your hands stop, and your breathing stops with them, for about a second. That freeze was never built for traffic or for exams. It was built for something that hunts movement, and forty thousand years later it is still the first thing you do.

Eleven minutes.

Now the turn.

Because the wolf in the grass is not extinct anymore.

In 2025 Colossal revealed three white wolf pups named Romulus, Remus and Khaleesi. Colossal calls them dire wolves. Plenty of scientists disagree, and call them grey wolves with a few edited genes. That argument is still running tonight. What nobody argues about is the pups. They exist. They eat. Colossal says they live on a preserve of more than two thousand acres. That is roughly a thousand football pitches of fenced grass.

In every world you walked through just now, you were the intruder. Wrong lungs, wrong instincts, wrong century.

Turn it around and it is the same story. Those pups do not belong here either. Their winter is gone. Their prey is gone. We pulled the world out from under them, and now we are building a fence and inviting them into ours.

You lasted three weeks in the first of their worlds and eleven minutes in the last one. Three white pups now have to last in yours. They get fenced grass and no winter. Nobody can tell them what they were brought back for. You will be alive to see how that goes.

So that's your scoreboard. Three weeks. Eleven days. Nine hours. Eleven minutes.

And here's the strange part. Out of those four worlds, the one everybody is actually trying to rebuild right now is the nine-hour one. The mammoth steppe. That's the era with a company behind it, a plan, and a countdown.

Except the mammoth they have made so far isn't a mammoth. It's a mouse. A living, breathing lab mouse with a handful of edited genes and thick golden fur, and it was announced to the world as a step toward the animal that used to walk that steppe.

I spent a whole video on that mouse — what is actually in it, what isn't, and why the gap between that animal and a real mammoth is bigger than the headlines made it sound. If you want to know how close the nine-hour era really is, that's the one to watch next. It's the difference between an animal coming back and a photo of one.

Watch the mammoth mouse video, then come back and tell me which of the four you'd survive.

## Vad de tre manusrundorna rättade

1. **Runda 1:** alla tre delarna underkändes och skrevs om. Slutkritikern hittade sedan ett
   fel ingen enskild del kunde se — direktvargen förekom två gånger, som egen era och som
   final. Fyra djur utspridda på fem pinnar.
2. **Runda 2:** dubbletter bort (varg, Perri 2021, La Brea, gåshud), och fem
   gissningsreservationer på cirka 130 ord ersatta av en enda tidig rad:
   *"Nobody has run the experiment. Every number in this video is a guess."*
3. **Runda 3:** åldersstegen bröts vid sista stoppet — ordningen var 1936 → 1662 → 20 000 →
   13 000 år, alltså ett steg *framåt* i tiden. La Brea-eran omdaterad till **fyrtiotusen
   år**, vilket ligger mitt i tjärgroparnas fångstintervall (cirka 50 000–11 000 år).
   Generationskedjan räknades om från femhundra till femtonhundra mödrar.
4. **Efter runda 3**, tre riktade fixar: de två efterhängda gissningstaggarna bort
   (paraplyvarningen täcker alla fyra ändå), fossilräkningen omskriven så inget barn läser
   den som en huvudräkning på plats, och femtonhundra mödrar fick en bild ögat rymmer.
