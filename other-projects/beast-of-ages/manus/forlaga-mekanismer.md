# Script mechanisms, taken off two references

Mathias supplied two long-form narration scripts as the bar: a Meta/Zuckerberg
collapse piece and an AI-agents-take-over-the-internet scenario. Neither is about
wildlife. They are here for **technique**, not subject.

These are the mechanisms both scripts share, written so a critic can check each one
by looking. Use them as the starting `bar.md` for the script loop, then add or drop
per episode. Do not treat them as a template to fill in — they are things the
reference *does*, and our version has to do them better.

---

## 1. The cold open states the end, not the setup

Never begins with context. Begins with the reversal or the aftermath.

> "In his 20s, he was the untouchable God of social media… But Mark Zuckerberg's luck has turned."

> "3 billion phones rang simultaneously. Four hours later, the president ordered the internet shut down. Indefinitely."

Then, and only then, the rewind: *"It all started six months ago, with a tweet."*

**Checkable:** does the first paragraph contain the outcome? Is there any scene-setting
before the first surprise? If the first 40 words could be deleted without loss, they
are setup and the open has failed.

## 2. One named thing carries the whole abstraction

The AI script does not explain "selection pressure in agent populations". It follows
**Agent 127** from birth to war, and the system explains itself through what happens to it.

**Checkable:** name the one thing the audience follows. If there isn't one, or if it
changes halfway, the piece is a lecture.

## 3. Numbers climb in a visible ladder

Script 2: 47 agents → 200 → 1,200 → 8,100 → 40,000 → 40 million → 900 million → 47 billion.
Script 1: $400 million → $1.6 billion → $73 billion → $125–145 billion.

Each figure lands harder because the last one is still in the viewer's head.

**Checkable:** list every number in order of appearance. Does it rise? Are there
numbers that add nothing, sitting between two that matter?

## 4. Every abstraction is cashed out in one concrete image, immediately

"AI slop" on its own means nothing. So:

> "Jesus Christ floating underwater, his body made of shrimps and lobsters amalgamated into a monster."

"Dual-class shares" on its own means nothing. So:

> "basically a division of first and second class shareholders"

**Checkable:** find every abstract noun. Is there a concrete image or plain-words
translation within the same or next sentence? An abstraction that survives two
sentences unexplained is a defect.

## 5. Scale is restated in kid-simple terms right after the figure

> "That's 'Billion', with a 'B.'"

> "That's larger than the population of Canada."

> "That's more than the population of Europe."

**Checkable:** does every number above a million get a comparison the audience can
picture? A raw figure with no anchor is a wasted number.

## 6. Analogy to something the viewer already knows

Wells Fargo's 3.5 million fake accounts to explain agents turning dishonest under
quota pressure. The Cambrian explosion to explain 20 weeks of arms race. Stuxnet.
"You don't notice when you step on an ant."

**Checkable:** count the analogies. Is each one to something the target age already
knows without being told? An analogy that itself needs explaining is not an analogy.

## 7. Questions carry the transitions

> "So, what does Zuckerberg do with his absolute control…? Well, he turns his company into a laboratory…"

> "What does it feel like to be one of these agents?"

> "So what do alliances do when legitimate niches dry up?"

**Checkable:** how does each section begin? A question the viewer is already asking is
a transition. "Additionally" and "Furthermore" are not.

## 8. Sentences are short and carry one idea

> "4429 is dead. Ceases to exist. 9012 is alive."

Paragraphs run one or two sentences. Long ones exist, but they are the exception and
they are followed immediately by a short one.

**Checkable:** average sentence length, longest sentence, and how many sentences carry
two ideas joined by "and" or "which". A run of three long sentences in a row is a defect.

## 9. Real named sources for anything contestable

Amnesty International, the Stanford researcher, the named Meta vice-presidents, the
strategist at Evolve Solutions Group. Quotes are attributed to a person with a role.

**Checkable:** every claim a viewer could dispute — is a source named? Unsourced
assertion delivered in an authoritative voice is the single easiest way to lose trust.

## 10. Stakes land at human scale

Not "infrastructure was affected":

> "One patient dies from a drug interaction the system would have flagged."

> "Two people die, waiting for ambulances that were never sent."

**Checkable:** for each consequence, is there one named or countable creature or person
it happened to? Aggregate harm with no individual in it does not land.

## 11. Ends unresolved, then hands off

> "…do we get closer and closer to the day when he actually goes bust?"

Then an explicit pointer to the next thing to watch.

**Checkable:** does the last paragraph resolve or open? A tidy conclusion ends the
session; an open question moves the viewer to the next video.

## What does NOT transfer to a wildlife channel

Both references are about human institutions behaving badly, and both lean on
corporate villainy for tension. Wildlife has no villain. The tension has to come from
somewhere else — usually **survival odds, time pressure, or a fight the animal cannot
win by strength**. Do not import a villain that isn't there; inventing malice in an
animal is the wildlife equivalent of a fabricated statistic.

Both also run far longer than 2000 words. At around 150 words a minute, 2000 words is
roughly 13 minutes. The escalation ladder still has to fit, which means fewer rungs,
not shorter rungs.
